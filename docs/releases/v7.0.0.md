# v7.0.0: Voice OS on your phone

Voice OS now lives on your phone as an app on the home screen. It has a layout made for one hand, it
stays signed in, and only one screen at a time listens and speaks, whichever device you picked up
last.

## Put it on your phone

Open **Settings → Your phone** on the Mac and press **New code**. Scan the QR code with the phone's
camera. The link carries a key of the phone's own, so it opens signed in. Safari shows how to add it to
the home screen, and Android gets an Install button. crew keeps only a hash of each key. **Revoke**
next to a phone, or `crew server phones rm <id>`, cuts it off on its next request, open connections
included.

Tailscale is the way I recommend getting there. Only your own devices reach Voice OS, it works away
from home, and the phone gets a real certificate. The **Reach it with Tailscale** card in Settings is a
checklist that ends with **Set it up**, which runs `crew server tailscale serve` for you. Through
crew's proxy instead, the phone has to trust crew's certificate first, and it only works on the same
network.

## A layout for the phone

**Home** is a list of your active sessions, grouped like your groups on the desktop. Each row has the
session's initials and state, how long it has been working or waiting, and one line of where it
stands. The one waiting on you comes first, with a red badge.

A session is only its conversation. A question, a plan or a permission sits at the end of it, right
above your thumb. **⋯** opens everything the desktop keeps beside the stream: status, cost, the mode,
model and context chips, dev servers with their logs, notes and the other sessions. **Done** closes
it.

The bar at the bottom follows the listening mode, which the round button on its left picks: hold the
mic for push to talk, nothing to press for hands-free and on demand, a send button for dictation, and
a new **Text only** mode with a message box and no microphone at all.

It moves like an app. Screens slide in and out, a swipe from the left edge goes back in the installed
iPhone app with Home showing underneath, and the phone's own back gesture works too. The page never
scrolls on its own and fits the screen when the keyboard is up. Settings on the phone is one scroll
of groups, like the phone's own.

## Listening and speaking on a phone

The phone asks for the microphone inside your tap, which iOS needs. Echo cancellation and noise
suppression are on in every mode, and in hands-free and on demand the mic sends nothing while Voice OS
speaks, so it never hears itself.

Speech plays even with the phone on silent, as a call would, and it no longer goes quiet when the mic
opens or closes. When the phone comes back from the lock screen, Voice OS wakes its audio again. What
was said while the phone was locked stays in the conversation.

## One main screen

The screen you open Voice OS on last is the main one: it listens and speaks. Every other tab and
device shows "Voice OS is open on iPhone" with **Use here**. A phone back from sleep keeps its place,
a duplicated tab doesn't steal it, and after a restart only the former main screen claims it again.

## Sessions asking sessions

Claude Code no longer has Grep or Glob tools, so the read-only copy that answers another session's
question could only open files it already knew the name of. It now has a search of its own,
`search_files`, which looks inside the session's folders and its Claude memory and never shows a
secret. The copy can read the session's memory folder too, so it answers from what the session knows
between conversations.

## For debugging

The page writes what happened to the microphone and the speech into the Voice OS log, so a phone
nobody can open developer tools on still says what went wrong: `crew server logs --cat=client`.

## Docs

Five tutorials walk through the page on a demo store, from a fresh install to working from your
phone, with every screenshot taken from Voice OS itself.
